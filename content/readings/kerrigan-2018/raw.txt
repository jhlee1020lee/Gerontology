Targetting Mutant mtDNA: Repairing Aging Cells In November of 2016, researchers at CalTech and UCLA (https:/ /interestingengineering.com/researchers-unlock-how-to-repair-aging- cells) discovered a method of manipulating the mitochondria of cells to effectively repair DNA. Cells typically contain two types of DNA found in the mitochondria - normal mtDNA and mutant mtDNA. The study noted that the build-up of mutant mtDNA over time causes cells to age and eventually die. The study was concerned with whether or not autophagy, or the process through which cells eat themselves, could be used to target the mutant mtDNA and prevent the aging process. ADVERTISEMENT They were successful in increasing mitophagy activity in a fruit y, noting a distinct reduction of mutant mtDNA in its muscle cells. Time will tell if similar techniques can be used to reduce aging or repair aged cells in humans. SCIENCE (HTTPS://INTERESTINGENGINEERING.COM/SCIENCE)

Activating Splicing Factors: Crafting Reversalogues to Encourage Cell Division In late 2017, BMC Cell Biology (https:/ /www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/320017.php) published research from the University of Exeter and the University of Brighton, both in the United Kingdom, which presented a breakthrough in reverse aging technology. The work built upon a previous University of Exeter discovery, which found that splicing factors in cells become inactive with age. The study found that by introducing reversalogues, similar to the chemical resveratrol which is found in red wine, splicing factors could be reactivated in older cells. This meant that the cells would continue to divide like younger cells, rejuvenating the cells and preventing cell aging and death. Smoothing Cells: Using Viruses to Smoothen Cell Wrinkles It's not just your skin that wrinkles as you age, turns out our cells get wrinkly too. In May of this year, researchers from the University of Virginia School of Medicine (https:/ /www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/05/180529103541.htm) found that many effects of aging, such as fatty liver disease, could be the result of cell nuclei wrinkling. They believe that viruses could be modi ed to carry and deliver lamin - a protein that can smooth the cells.
