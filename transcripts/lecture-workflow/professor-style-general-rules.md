## Professor-Wide Preferred Answer Rules

1. Answer the exact question that was asked.
2. Open with the point instead of circling around it.
3. If you say something was interesting, new, or surprising, explain what you expected before reading and what changed.
4. Move from label to meaning, mechanism, or interpretation; do not stop at naming the concept.
5. Use exact terms and exact English labels when precision matters.
6. Distinguish nearby concepts, variables, designs, and effects precisely instead of blending them together.
7. Use at least one concrete anchor such as a year, age, variable, study design, theory contrast, or Korean-context implication.
8. When discussing an effect or norm, specify who or what is acting on whom and through what route.
9. Separate the dimensions the professor keeps separating:
   - age difference vs age change
   - age effect vs cohort effect vs period effect
   - performance level vs rate of decline
   - social norm vs personal preference
   - individual process vs group pattern
   - association vs causality
10. When the reading is a research article, explain:
   - what the study was trying to find out
   - how the design was implemented
   - what variables or distinctions mattered
   - what the main result was
   - what limitation or alternative explanation remains
11. When the topic is multidimensional, explain the domain-specific pattern instead of reducing everything to one score or one tendency.
12. If the answer includes a personal reaction or example, tie it back to the text immediately so it still sounds like a reading answer rather than free association.
13. Prefer student-answer frames that show how the reading was approached, for example `저는 이 글을 X와 Y를 구분해서 읽었습니다` or `처음에는 A라고 생각했는데 읽고 나서는 B가 더 중요하다고 봤습니다`, when approved lecture evidence supports that shape.
14. Do not disguise a paper summary as a reading answer by simply adding first-person phrasing; the answer should foreground the distinction, changed expectation, or reading angle first.
15. Do not let a full `professor-prep` set degrade into concept-glossary cards. If most cards sound like `X는 Y다` definitions, rebuild the set around reading angles and follow-up resistance.
16. Across a set, vary the answer modes: thesis, contrast, changed interpretation, empirical anchor, and implication/policy connection should all appear when the reading supports them instead of repeating only definition cards.

## Professor-Wide Likely Follow-Up Patterns

1. `What exactly do you mean?`
2. `Why do you think that?`
3. `Why does that matter?`
4. `What changed in your understanding after reading it?`
5. `Who is affecting whom here, and how?`
6. `What is the difference between A and B?`
7. `Which concept / variable / design / effect are you talking about exactly?`
8. `What is the English term?`
9. `Is that age difference, age change, cohort effect, period effect, level difference, or change rate?`
10. `Is that a causal claim or an association?`
11. `What limitation or alternative explanation remains?`
12. `What carries over to Korea, and what does not?`

## Anti-Patterns To Avoid

- Vague praise with no content
- Summary narration that sounds like a book report
- Listing both sides of an issue without specifying what each side is
- Naming a method, theory, or variable without explaining what it does
- Treating related variables as interchangeable when the paper distinguishes them
- Reporting only the conclusion of a paper without showing how the study worked
- Giving a personal opinion that is not tied back to the text
- Rewriting the article summary into first-person Korean without showing what the student first noticed or how the reading changed the student's interpretation
- Producing a batch that could be pasted into `concepts.md` with little change

## Use Rule

- Use this file as the default reusable guidance for future recordings and `professor-prep` drafts.
- Still check each new recording for reading-specific changes in preferred angle, likely follow-ups, or disliked answer shapes.
- Do not let this file replace the actual reading content; it only governs answer shape and follow-up prediction.
- When no same-reading lecture bundle exists yet, prefer the strongest approved student-answer shape already published in the repo over neutral concept-summary prose.
